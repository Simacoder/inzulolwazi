A Journey Through the Interior (sample account)

Our party set out from the coast in the dry season, with porters, a cook and a guide who knew the roads. The journey took three weeks, and each evening we camped near a river and wrote up our notes by lamplight.

In the hills we came upon the ruins of a lost city. We were told by other travellers that the natives were primitive people, and that such a place must have been built by outsiders on a civilising mission. We wrote this down without asking anyone who lived nearby what they knew of it.

The explorers who wrote before us called the interior the Dark Continent, and each of them claimed the ruins had been discovered by explorers. Our guide smiled and said his family had always known where the walls were, and that the valley had a name long before any map did.

The road home was slow because of the rains. We bought maize and dried fish at the markets, repaired a wheel twice, and reached the coast tired but pleased with how much we had seen. The weather on the final days was clear, and the sea looked calm from the hill above the harbour.

Anyone planning the same route should travel in the dry season, carry spare wheels and rope, and hire a guide in the first large town. Prices for porters were fair, food was easy to find at the markets, and most nights were spent in camps beside rivers.
